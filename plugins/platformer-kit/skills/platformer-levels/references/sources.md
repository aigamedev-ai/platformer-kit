# Where these rules come from

These rules are our own summary of how Celeste's and Super Mario's levels were designed, plus common difficulty curve practice. They are not quotes.

- Maddy Thorson, "Level Design Workshop: Designing Celeste", GDC 2017. Covers rooms built around a short story, more than one way through, making impossible routes obviously impossible, safety and pacing, short rooms, and teaching through level design instead of text. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4RlpMhBKNr0
- Casey Jarmes, "Celeste Level Design Case Study" (2020). Covers safe first encounters, warnings before hazards and the gradual introduction of each mechanic. https://caseyjarmes.wordpress.com/2020/09/15/celeste-level-design-case-study/
- Joseph Diamond, "The award-winning level design in Celeste". Covers readable affordances and alternating movement with planning pauses. https://medium.com/@josephdiamond115/the-award-winning-level-design-in-celeste-c2acb315bf79
- Koichi Hayashida (director, Super Mario 3D Land) on kishōtenketsu in Mario level design: introduce, develop, twist, conclude. https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design
- On difficulty curve shapes, including the sawtooth: https://www.abratabia.com/game-design/difficulty-curves.php
