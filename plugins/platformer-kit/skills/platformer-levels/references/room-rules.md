# One room, one story

A room's story is one sentence about what the player does, e.g.:

- "Walk off the low step: falling doesn't hurt."
- "Jump the spike pit, then double jump up to the ledge."
- "Hop across three short platforms with no rest in between."

**How to use it**

1. Write the sentence before placing tiles.
2. Make the story part the only demanding part. Make the entrance and exit generous: wide platforms, low drops, no hazards near the door.
3. After building, look for shortcuts that skip the story. If one exists, either block it so it is clearly blocked (a wall, a raised spike row, a gap far beyond reach) or accept it as a valid other way.
4. If a change makes the room better but breaks the sentence, you are now making a different room. Decide which room you want.
5. If the story is dull, cut the room. Fewer good rooms beat more filler.

**Room size:** one screen is the default. Longer rooms raise the stakes, because a death costs more. Use them rarely and make their start quick to replay.

**Pacing across rooms:** alternate tension and rest. After a hard room, give an easy one. Put the hardest room near the end, followed by a short calm room before the goal.
